# FairNest UK

**Business feasibility of an evidence-led accommodation decision platform for international students — Coventry pilot**

FairNest UK is a postgraduate International Business Management project that examines whether a source-transparent accommodation decision-support service can improve comparison of student housing options. The pilot focuses on Coventry and uses public secondary evidence rather than primary participant data.

## Repository contents

- `artifact/FairNest_Decision_Engine.html` — functional browser-based prototype.
- `evidence/FairNest_Evidence_and_Financial_Model.xlsx` — source register, Coventry listing data, competitor audit, regulatory controls and financial scenarios.
- `evidence/*.csv` — Git-readable extracts of the main evidence tables.
- `report/FairNest_UK_Final_Report.docx` — final academic report.
- `docs/methodology_traceability.md` — concise explanation of the evidence-to-design chain.
- `docs/data_dictionary.md` — field definitions for the main evidence files.

## Research scope

The project is a Coventry proof of concept. It does not claim national validation, observed student willingness to pay or live provider adoption. The evidence base combines official statistics and regulation, peer-reviewed research, first-party accommodation data, specialist market intelligence and current competitor/platform information.

## Core decision logic

The prototype separates headline weekly rent, total contract cost, known booking or move-in cash, commute, agreement context and evidence completeness.

The Fit Score is a user-controlled preference measure, not a safety, legal-compliance or quality certificate.

## Running the artefact

No installation is required. Open `artifact/FairNest_Decision_Engine.html` in a modern browser.

## Evidence and reproducibility

The Excel workbook is the authoritative analytical file because it retains formulas and assumptions. CSV extracts are included only to make the key evidence tables readable and diffable on GitHub.

Commercial pricing assumptions in the financial model are explicitly treated as managerial assumptions rather than observed willingness-to-pay evidence.
