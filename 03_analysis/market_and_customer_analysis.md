# Market and customer analysis

## Market size without a growth assumption
HESA's 2024/25 statistics confirm a large UK higher-education market but also show weaker international demand. FairNest therefore cannot rely on a narrative that international enrolment will automatically rise every year.

## Accommodation cost as a decision driver
Office for Students research supports the salience of accommodation cost. The project uses this evidence to justify making total contract cost and known booking cash visible rather than relying on weekly headline rent.

## Coventry as a useful pilot
Coventry City Council's PBSA market study reports:
- demand pool: 41,835;
- PBSA beds: 25,590;
- five-year demand-pool growth: 13%;
- five-year PBSA-bed growth: 71%;
- student-to-bed ratio: 1.62:1;
- nationally observed reference: 2.12:1.

The study interpreted the Coventry ratio as relative oversupply. This weakens a scarcity-based proposition but strengthens the argument for better comparison in a market with many options.

## Customer problem
The pilot focuses on four recurring decision frictions:
1. headline weekly rent can hide the immediate cash requirement;
2. contractual/payment context differs across housing types;
3. evidence quality varies across provider and aggregator pages;
4. students and sponsors may value different trade-offs.

## Strategic implication
FairNest should compete on **decision quality and traceability**, not inventory volume.
