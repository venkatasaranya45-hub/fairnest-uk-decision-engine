# Research method and ethics

## Research design
A pragmatic secondary-data feasibility study was used because the project needed to integrate market evidence, regulation, academic literature, competitor features and first-party accommodation data into one business decision.

## Evidence selection
The evidence hierarchy was:
1. official statistics, regulators and government;
2. peer-reviewed scholarship;
3. first-party institutional/provider data;
4. specialist market intelligence;
5. live commercial-platform evidence for competitor functionality.

Sources were retained only where their role in the analysis was clear and their limitations could be stated.

## Data extraction
The project recorded:
- source identity and URL/DOI;
- evidence type;
- claim or field used;
- evidence-check date where relevant;
- limitation/transfer issue;
- whether the evidence informed the report, model or artefact.

## Reliability and validity controls
- exact first-party pages were preferred for room prices and payment terms;
- competitor claims were based on reviewed features rather than marketing slogans;
- missing values remained missing rather than being imputed as zero;
- commercial prices in the financial model were labelled as assumptions;
- international evidence from Australia/Scotland was used for mechanisms, not UK prevalence claims;
- regulatory prompts were bounded by agreement context.

## Ethics
The completed project uses secondary data and public organisational information. It does not report interviews, surveys or focus groups. The prototype does not require protected-characteristic data for ranking.

Before a live service, FairNest would still need documented lawful bases, privacy information, retention rules and operational data-protection controls.

## Legal boundary
FairNest provides decision support and links to authoritative guidance. It does not provide individual legal advice.
