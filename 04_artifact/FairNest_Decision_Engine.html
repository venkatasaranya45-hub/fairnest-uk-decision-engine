Contract & legal check

FairNest does not assume that every student accommodation agreement is governed by the same legal rules. The first control is therefore agreement classification. The flags below are decision prompts linked to official 2026 guidance, not legal advice.

Private assured periodic tenancy (England)
From 1 May 2026 most private assured tenancies are rolling rather than fixed-term. A private landlord/agent cannot ask for or accept rent before the agreement is signed; after signing, the tenant can normally be asked for no more than one month’s rent in advance.
Rule-based check
Holding deposit
For an assured tenancy, the holding deposit is capped at one week’s rent.
Check amount
Tenancy/security deposit
Normally capped at five weeks’ rent where annual rent is below £50,000 (six weeks at £50,000–£100,000) and must be protected in an approved scheme when the rules apply.
Check amount + scheme
University halls / licence
Government guidance explicitly says different rules apply to student halls of residence. FairNest therefore displays the provider’s licence/payment terms rather than applying private-tenancy caps automatically.
Do not generalise
Private PBSA
The legal form can differ. The user must verify whether the agreement is a licence, common-law tenancy or an assured tenancy before FairNest applies tenancy-specific legal checks.
Classification required
Right to rent
Private landlords in England normally conduct right-to-rent checks. UKCISA notes exemptions for some student halls and institution-managed/nominated accommodation.
Context specific

Primary regulatory sources: GOV.UK Renters’ Rights Act guidance; Tenant Fees Act guidance; UKCISA accommodation guidance. FairNest should link users to official advice for disputes or individual legal questions.

Evidence provenance

High-confidence fields come from an official university/provider page that names the room, contract and price. Aggregator “from” prices are intentionally scored lower because the room, contract length or payment schedule may not be explicit. This prevents missing information from looking equivalent to verified information.

Parent / sponsor decision summary

This view turns the student’s shortlist into the questions that usually matter before money is transferred.

Before paying

  1. Confirm the exact room, academic year and contract length on the provider’s own page.
  2. Check the amount required immediately, not only the weekly headline rent.
  3. Identify the agreement type before applying private-tenancy legal rules.
  4. For private renting, check the holding deposit, tenancy deposit protection and rent-in-advance rules.
  5. Do not send money to a person or account solely because a social-media advert looks genuine; use provider/institution contact details from an independent source.

FairNest is a comparison and evidence tool. It does not certify that a property is risk-free, guarantee availability, or replace university, Citizens Advice, UKCISA or qualified legal support.

How the Fit Score works

The score is deliberately user-controlled and explainable. It does not embed nationality, ethnicity, religion, visa nationality, or any protected characteristic. Paid provider status must never change score or ordering.

Four component scores

  • Recurring-cost fit: 100 when the weekly price is at or below the user budget; above budget it declines proportionally.
  • Move-in-cash fit: 100 when the known upfront amount is within the user limit. Unknown payment data receives a neutral-low score rather than being treated as zero.
  • Commute fit: highest inside the user’s stated walk threshold and falls when the option exceeds it.
  • Evidence completeness: pre-scored from availability of price, contract length, bills, upfront payment, location/commute and provider-source information.

Formula

Fit = Σ(normalised user weight × component score). Changing the sliders immediately changes the ordering. This is important because “best” accommodation is preference-dependent. The system therefore exposes trade-offs rather than asserting a universal best property.

Design controls

Sponsored placements, if introduced, would be visually separated and labelled “Paid placement”; sponsored status would not feed the Fit Score. Evidence has a check date and source link. Missing data is visible. Price and availability are snapshots and must be rechecked before payment.
